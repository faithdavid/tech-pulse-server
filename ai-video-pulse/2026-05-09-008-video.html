Camera Vocabulary for AI Video — The 7 Dimensions Every Pro Should Know

Cinematographer-grade camera vocabulary that AI models actually understand and translate into correct geometry. Seven dimensions: (1) Camera Height — high angle (vulnerability), low angle (power), eye level (neutral), ground level, worm's eye (exaggerated scale), overhead. (2) Camera Distance — ECU (eyes/mouth detail), CU (face fills frame), MCU (shoulders up), MS (waist up), MWS (knees up), WS (full body), ELS (tiny subject in massive landscape). (3) Camera Orientation — straight-on, three-quarter (45°), profile (90°), dutch angle (tension), back-view. (4) Position — over-the-shoulder (OTS), POV, reverse angle, frontal symmetrical (Kubrick-style). (5) Perspective — wide-angle (expanded space), telephoto (compression), anamorphic (cinematic oval bokeh), fisheye (180° field). (6) 3D Space — front-facing, off-axis left/right, high vantage, close foreground placement. (7) Camera Movement — push-in, pull-back, lateral tracking, arc shot, tilt-up/down, handheld wobble. Layer these in your prompts: 'Low angle MCU tracking right, anamorphic perspective, golden hour light' and watch the models deliver exactly what you asked for.

The 8 Control Layers Framework — Stop Vibes-Based Prompting

Pro AI filmmakers now use Technical Orchestration with 8 control layers instead of vague prompting. Layer 1: Subject/Scene — who and where. Layer 2: Emotion Arc — explicit emotional tokens ('curiosity turning to wonder'). Layer 3: Optics — lens and aperture ('35mm anamorphic f/2.8'). Layer 4: Camera Motion — precise vectors ('dolly-in at 0.5m/s'). Layer 5: Lighting Stack — key + fill + rim with color temperatures ('5600K key, 3200K rim'). Layer 6: Style/Look — film emulation ('Kodak Portra 400 aesthetic'). Layer 7: Audio/Mood — cues for generative audio. Layer 8: Continuity Anchors — seeds, palettes, wardrobe tokens. Practice by taking one real scene and filling out all 8 layers before generating — you'll see 5x fewer reshoots.

Cinematic Lighting Prompts That Actually Work — 20 Real Examples

Lighting is single-handedly the fastest way to make AI video look professional vs amateur. Key terms every AI video prompt needs: (1) Key Light — main light source, sets the mood (hard key = dramatic shadows, soft key = flattering). (2) Fill Light — fills in shadows, controls contrast ratio (low fill = noir, high fill = commercial). (3) Backlight/Rim Light — separates subject from background, creates depth. (4) Practical Light — sources visible in frame (lamps, candles, neon signs). (5) Color Temperature — warm (3200K tungsten) = cozy/intimate, cool (5600K daylight) = clinical/alert. (6) Gobo/Texture — patterned light through blinds, leaves, water. Prompt formula: '[Lighting Setup] + [Quality] + [Direction] + [Color]' — example: 'Rim-lit with a warm tungsten key from camera right, cool fill from ambient moonlight, hard shadows, cinematic volumetric haze.'
